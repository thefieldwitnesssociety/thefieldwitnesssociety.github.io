About the Society

Geography, exploration and documentary practice.

An independent international society whose work begins in the field.

Purpose

The Field Witness Society publishes work produced through direct engagement with place. Photography, writing and geographical context come together in records of landscapes, environments and the systems that connect them.

The Society is developing its first programme of Field Notes. Its opening publication follows Mauritania’s iron ore railway from Choum towards the Atlantic coast.

Mission

The world does not need to be discovered.

It needs to be understood more deeply.

The Field Witness Society exists to advance a contemporary practice of geographical exploration grounded in presence, observation and documentation.

It supports, publishes and connects work produced through direct engagement with place, encouraging field practice that combines intellectual curiosity with accuracy, context and restraint.

Its purpose is to deepen the understanding of landscapes, environments, communities and systems through work that begins in the field.

Field practice

Field Notes combine photographs and writing with the information needed to understand them. Each publication identifies its author, location and dates, explains the context, and acknowledges the limits of the record.

Observation and interpretation have different roles. Captions describe what the photographs show; external sources provide context where it is needed. A field record can leave questions open.

Principles

  1. Presence before interpretation

    Understanding begins with being there. Direct experience does not guarantee understanding, but meaningful field practice begins with attention to the place itself.

  2. Truth before spectacle

    The quality of a record is more important than its dramatic effect. Observation should not be distorted in order to make a place appear more remote, dangerous, exotic or extraordinary than it is.

  3. Context matters

    A photograph, observation or story acquires meaning through geography, history, conditions, relationships and scale. Description and situation should precede broad claims.

  4. Explore without conquest

    Exploration is an act of inquiry, not possession. The Society rejects the language and mythology of conquest, domination and the supposedly “untouched”.

  5. Observe with respect

    Presence carries obligations. People are participants in places, not visual material. Accuracy, consent, proportion and respect for uncertainty belong to responsible witnessing.

  6. Document with purpose

    A field record should contribute something beyond proof of presence. Photography, writing, film, maps and data should clarify, preserve, question or deepen understanding.

  7. Remain curious

    A Field Witness is not required to arrive with certainty. Questions, ambiguity and incomplete knowledge are legitimate parts of serious observation.
