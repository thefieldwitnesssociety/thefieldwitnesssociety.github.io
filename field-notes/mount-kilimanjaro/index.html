Field Note 005 / Mount Kilimanjaro, Tanzania

Mount Kilimanjaro

Five days on the Marangu Route

1–5 July 2024Expedition with Guglielmo Araldi, Umberto Pernice, Tommaso Bruno and Sam Thompson

Kilimanjaro rises in northern Tanzania as an isolated volcanic massif. Its three principal volcanic peaks — Kibo, Mawenzi and Shira — form Africa’s highest mountain, reaching 5,895 metres at Uhuru Peak.

This record follows five days on the Marangu Route, from 1 to 5 July 2024. Guglielmo Araldi, Umberto Pernice, Tommaso Bruno and Sam Thompson spent approximately three and a half days ascending and one and a half days descending, moving through forest, heath and moorland, alpine desert and the summit zone.

A mountain in layers

The Marangu Route approaches the upper mountain from the southeast. Gaining altitude also means moving through successive changes in vegetation, temperature and exposure. Dense forest gives way to open heath, then to sparse volcanic ground.

The route is experienced at two different speeds: gradually on the ascent, then rapidly in reverse. The 3.5-day ascent and 1.5-day descent give the field record its rhythm.

From forest to open ground

The lower route is enclosed by montane forest. Mist, dense vegetation and the narrow path compress the view; higher up, the canopy opens into heath and moorland. The photographs begin to carry more sky. Rest points, signs and other walkers give the route a human scale.

The alpine desert

Above the vegetation, the route becomes spare. Rock, dust and low plants replace the enclosed lower slopes. The air is colder and drier, and cloud can erase the mountain within minutes before opening again onto a wider horizon.

Kibo is the central and highest volcanic edifice; Mawenzi is the sharply dissected peak to the east; Shira forms the older western shoulder. At this height, geology becomes the dominant surface.

The hours before light

The summit push begins in darkness. The visible world contracts to headlamps, boots, packs and a few metres of ground. Pace slows as the route climbs towards the crater rim. Near the top, darkness separates into colour and a thin horizon appears over the upper mountain.

Uhuru Peak

Uhuru Peak stands at 5,895 metres on Kibo, the highest point in Africa. The summit marks the end of the ascent, but not the end of the route. In roughly a day and a half, the layers accumulated during the climb are crossed again in reverse.
